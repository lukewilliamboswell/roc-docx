= Read a document's tracked changes

This chapter reads the changes a document records under tracking: what was
inserted, deleted or moved, which paragraph marks, table rows and cells were
added or removed, which properties changed, by whom and when; the
document's text with every change accepted or every change rejected; and
whether the document tracks changes at all. It accepts and rejects changes,
edits a document's text silently or as tracked changes, turns tracking on
and off, and says how the changes are written. It continues from
<<read-a-documents-text>>, whose blocks and current text it builds on.

== The text before and after review

`Docx.final_text` gives the body's text with every tracked change accepted,
and `Docx.original_text` with every change rejected:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=review-texts,indent=0]
----

Both read the body the way `Docx.text` does, every paragraph's text followed
by a line feed, and differ in what the changes leave:

* In the final text, inserted text and text moved in are included, and
  deleted text and text moved away are not. A paragraph whose mark was
  deleted or moved away runs on into the next paragraph, and deleted table
  rows and cells are left out.
* In the original text, deleted text and text moved away are included, and
  inserted text and text moved in are not. A paragraph whose mark was
  inserted or moved in runs on into the next paragraph, and inserted table
  rows and cells are left out.

A paragraph never runs on into or out of a table cell. A deleted field
contributes the result it last displayed, as any field does; its deleted
instructions contribute nothing.

`Docx.text` is the current text, as LibreOffice and pandoc show a document
with its changes as they stand. It is the final text except that a deleted
row or cell is still there, without the text deleted from it.

`Revisions.text` gives the same three texts of any blocks, such as a table
cell's, with `Current`, `Final` or `Original`.

== The changes

`Docx.revisions` lists the body's tracked changes in reading order,
including those in tables, content controls, hyperlinks and fields:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=revisions,indent=0]
----

Each entry is a `Revisions.Listed` record:

* `id`, the change's place among the body's changes in reading order, which
  identifies it; the document's own numbers for its changes are not part of
  the model;
* `kind`: `Insertion`, `Deletion`, `MoveSource` for content moved away from
  here, `MoveDestination` for the same content arriving elsewhere, `Merge`
  for a cell merged with the cell above, or `Formatting` for a change of
  properties;
* `of`, what changed: `Content` of a paragraph, a `ParagraphMark`, a `Row`
  or `Cell` of a table, or, for `Formatting`, the properties of a `Run`,
  `Paragraph`, `Section`, `Table`, `Row` or `Cell`;
* `author`, the name of the person who made the change;
* `date`, `Date(timestamp)` or `Unset`, a `Document.Timestamp` as a
  comment's date is, so `utc_seconds` gives the instant whatever zone the
  document wrote;
* `content`, the inlines the change covers: a content change's, the run a
  run's formatting changed, or the content of a paragraph whose formatting
  changed; the other changes cover none;
* `move`, `Move(name)` for a move's source or destination that stands
  inside a range of its side, naming the move, or `Unpaired`;
* `text`, the content's text, with the changes inside it shown, so a
  deletion's text is the text it deleted; a paragraph mark's change has a
  line feed, the text a paragraph ends with, and a change of a section, table,
  row or cell none;
* `follows`, whether the change comes directly after the one listed before
  it, nothing between them, as one change split in two does, or a change
  that runs on over a paragraph's mark into the next paragraph.

A change inside another, such as an insertion made inside text that was
later moved, is listed after the change that holds it. A paragraph's
formatting is listed before the changes of its content and its mark's
changes after them; a table's formatting before its rows, a row's insertion,
deletion and formatting before its cells, and a cell's insertion, deletion or
merge and its formatting before its content.

== Changes in the model

In a paragraph's content, a tracked change is a
`Revision({ kind, author, date, content, preserved })` inline: the change
and the inlines it covers. `preserved` keeps the change's other attributes,
and a date that is not a date and time, as they were read. Inside a deletion
or a move's source, deleted text is `Text` run content and deleted field
instructions are `FieldCode`, so deleted content reads like any other.

A move is recorded twice: where the content was and where it went. Each side
stands inside a range, `MoveStart({ side, name, author, date, preserved })`
to `MoveEnd({ side, name })`, with `side` either `Source` or `Destination`.
The source's and the destination's ranges share the move's name, which pairs
them. A range's `date` is a `Document.Timestamp` itself, not `Unset` or
`Date`, because the schema requires a move's range to say when it was made.

The schema requires every change to name its author. A change without one is
kept as a `Container` of its content rather than read with an invented
author, and its deleted text stays `Preserved`. A move's range without a
name, an author or a date and time, and its end, or an end that names no
range that started, is kept as `Preserved`.

A paragraph's mark, which ends it, is the subject of its own changes, held
in its properties as `mark`: a list of `{ kind, author, date, preserved }`,
at most one of each kind, in the order inserted, deleted, moved away, moved
here.
The mark's own character properties, and a change of them, stay in the
paragraph's `preserved` properties.

A table row's properties hold `inserted` and `deleted`, each
`Tracked({ author, date, preserved })` or `Unset`, and a
cell's properties hold `revision`: `Inserted`, `Deleted`, or `Merged` with
the vertical merge the change made and the one it replaced.

A change of properties is part of the properties it changed. The
properties of a paragraph, run, table, row, cell and section each have a
`change`, a list holding the change, `{ author, date, preserved, previous }`,
when there is one, where `previous` holds the properties as they were
before it. The properties before a change have no changes of their own:
their `change` is `Unset`, reading leaves their `mark` empty and keeps what
the schema does not allow there, such as a section's headers, in their
`preserved`, and writing does not write them.

A table's grid as it was before a change, which names no author, and a
row's table property exceptions with their changes are kept as they were
read, in the grid and the row, and written back unchanged.

== Accept and reject changes

`Docx.accept_all` accepts every tracked change and `Docx.reject_all` rejects
every one, in the body, the notes, the headers and footers and the comments,
each giving a new document:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=accept-all,indent=0]
----

The accepted document's text is the original's final text and the rejected
document's its original text, and neither has a change left. `Docx.accept`
and `Docx.reject` take the changes a `Revisions.Selection` selects, leaving
the others as they are:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=select,indent=0]
----

* `All` selects every change.
* `By(author)` selects the changes one author made.
* `Between({ from, until })` selects the changes made from `from` up to but
  not including `until`, both XML Schema dates and times compared as
  instants, so a change without a date is in no range.
* `Ids(ids)` selects the changes with these `id`s from `Docx.revisions`,
  which lists the body's changes, so it selects none in the notes, headers,
  footers and comments. Accepting or rejecting changes takes them out of the
  list, so the `id`s of the changes after them change: read `Docx.revisions`
  again before selecting by `id` once more.

What accepting and rejecting do, change by change:

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Change |Accepted |Rejected
|Inserted content, or content moved here |The content stays, without the change |The content goes
|Deleted content, or content moved away |The content goes |The content stays, without the change
|Changed properties |The properties stay; those before go |The properties before come back
|Inserted paragraph mark, or one moved here |The mark stays |The paragraph joins the next
|Deleted paragraph mark, or one moved away |The paragraph joins the next |The mark stays
|Inserted row or cell |It stays |It goes, with its content
|Deleted row or cell |It goes, with its content |It stays
|Merged cell |The merge stays |The cell's vertical merge before it comes back
|===

Content that goes leaves behind the comment and bookmark boundaries and the
comment references it held, so a comment on it keeps its place. A table left
without rows goes too. A move's ranges go once every change in them is
accepted or rejected.

A paragraph that joins the next gives that paragraph its content, at its
start, and the joined paragraph keeps the next paragraph's properties, since
the mark that held its own is gone. If the mark also ended a section, the
section's end goes with it and its text joins the next section, as in Word,
unless the section ends the body. A paragraph with no paragraph after it to
join before a table, the end of its cell or the end of the body stays as it
is. These are the rules `Revisions.text` follows, so the text of an accepted
document is always the final text, and of a rejected one the original text.

The changes of each note, header, footer and comment are resolved as the
body's are, each part on its own: a paragraph joins only the next paragraph
of its own note, header, footer or comment. Each edit gives the document as
`Docx.to_bytes` writes it. `Revisions.accept` and `Revisions.reject` do the
same to any blocks.

== Edit text as tracked changes

`Docx.replace_text`, `Docx.insert_text` and `Docx.delete_text` change a
document's text. Each takes a `Revisions.Context` that says how: `Untracked`
changes the text as it stands, and `Tracked({ author, date })` records the
edit as tracked changes by that author at that date, which the application
gives, since the package reads no clock. The same edit is never both.

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=replace-text,indent=0]
----

Tracked, the replaced text stays in the document as a deletion and the new
text follows it as an insertion, so accepting both gives the text edited
silently and rejecting both gives the text before the edit. Deleting text
that was inserted earlier puts a deletion inside that insertion.

An edit can run over the ends of paragraphs, and new text can end one and
begin another:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=across-lines,indent=0]
----

* A paragraph whose line feed is in the span joins the next paragraph, which
  keeps its own properties, as when a deleted paragraph mark is accepted;
  tracked, the paragraph's mark is deleted. If the mark ended a section, the
  section's end goes with it.
* A line feed in the new text splits the paragraph it lands in. The part
  before takes a copy of the paragraph's properties, so new paragraphs after
  a heading are headings too, and a new mark, inserted when tracked; the
  part after keeps the paragraph's own mark. Rejecting the inserted mark
  joins the parts again. A hyperlink, field or change the line feed lands
  in is split in two with the paragraph.

=== Where a text edit applies

A span, a `Comments.Span` as comments use, is `{ start, end }`: byte offsets
into the current text `Docx.text` gives. Deleted text is not current text,
so it has no width: offsets count inserted text and skip deleted text, and
an edit leaves text already deleted as it is. `Docx.insert_text` takes one
offset, the empty span at it, and `Docx.delete_text` replaces the span with
nothing.

* The span's characters go, and so does content with no text strictly
  inside it, such as a picture, a note reference or a field's marks;
  comment and bookmark boundaries and comment references stay.
* The new text goes just after the character before the span's end, inside
  the hyperlink, field or change that character is in unless the character
  ends it, and with that character's run properties; where the span ends at
  the start of a line, at the start of that paragraph, with its first run's
  properties. A tab in the new text is a tab.
* An edit changes no other text, so offsets before the span stay and those
  after it move by the difference in length. Making several edits from the
  last to the first keeps every offset taken from one text valid.

A line feed in a span must end a paragraph that has a paragraph after it to
join: a span holding the end of a table's row or cell, or the line feed of
a paragraph followed by a table or ending its cell or the body, fails with
`LineFeedInSpan(span)`. A span that is not within the text, ends before it
starts, or inserts after the last line fails with `OutsideText(span)`, one
that starts or ends inside a character with `NotACharacterBoundary(offset)`,
and a context whose date is not a date and time with `NotADate(text)`.

The `redline` example accepts one reviewer's changes and rejects another's,
then replaces, inserts and deletes text as tracked changes, one insertion
adding a paragraph, and writes the document with tracking on.

== Insert and delete paragraphs and tables

`Docx.insert_blocks` inserts blocks of the model where a paragraph or table
begins, at a byte offset of `Docx.text` that starts a line, or at the end of
the text, and `Docx.delete_blocks` deletes the paragraphs and tables from
where one begins to where one ends, with its line feed. Both take a
`Revisions.Context`. The new paragraph here is made as
<<build-the-content>> describes:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=block-edits,indent=0]
----

Where a table begins, new blocks go before the table, not into its first
cell; to insert into a cell, give the offset where one of its paragraphs
begins.

Tracked, a new paragraph's content is an insertion and its mark is inserted,
and a new table's rows are inserted with their cells' content. A deleted
paragraph's content and mark are deleted, and a deleted table's rows with
their cells' content. Rejecting takes inserted blocks away and accepting
takes deleted ones away, by the rules of <<accept-and-reject-changes>>, so
accepting a tracked block edit gives the same edit made silently and
rejecting it gives the document before:

* Rejecting an inserted mark joins its paragraph into the next, so new
  paragraphs need the paragraph they go before. At the end of the body, or
  before a table, the mark of the paragraph before them is inserted
  instead, as Word records a paragraph added at the end, and the last new
  paragraph, if its properties differ from that paragraph's, records them
  as a tracked change from them, so rejecting restores the paragraph before
  as it was.
* Accepting a deleted mark joins the paragraph into the next, so deleted
  paragraphs need a paragraph directly after them, which keeps its own
  properties and receives the comment and bookmark boundaries and comment
  references they held. Deleted untracked, they leave the same.

An insertion or deletion whose paragraphs would have no paragraph to join,
because the paragraph before has changes of its mark, there is none, or a
table stands between them, fails with `NoParagraphToJoin(offset)`, whether
tracked or not, so the same edit never succeeds one way and fails the other.
An offset where no block begins or ends fails with
`NotABlockBoundary(offset)`.

== Write tracked changes

`Docx.to_bytes` writes every change the model holds, as <<write-a-document>>
writes the rest of the body, so the changes a document is read with are the
changes it is written with. A change built in the model, such as a
`Revision` around new runs, is written the same way:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=model-revision,indent=0]
----

`stamp` is a `Document.Timestamp`, here from `WmlRead.date_time`; the date
and the author come from the application, never from the clock.

* A content change is written as an insertion, deletion or move element
  around its content, with its author, its date as the timestamp's
  `written` form and the attributes it kept. Inside a deletion, text is
  written as deleted text and field code as deleted field instructions.
* A move's ranges are written with their name, author and date, a range's
  end with its start's identifier.
* A paragraph mark's changes are written first in the mark's character
  properties, in the order inserted, deleted, moved away, moved here.
* A row's insertion and deletion and a cell's insertion, deletion or merge
  are written in the row's and cell's properties, where the schema puts
  them.
* A change of properties is written last in the properties it changed,
  around the properties as they were before it. Those are written without
  changes of their own and, for a section, without its headers and footers,
  which the schema has no place for there.
* The schema has no place for a hyperlink or a simple field inside a
  content change. A change that covers one is written around the content
  beside it and again inside it, around its content, so the document reads
  back with one change before, one inside and one after, each with the same
  kind, author and date.

Each change takes an identifier from the space the document's comments and
bookmarks share, the lowest left, in the order it is written. The tracking
setting is written as the document's settings hold it, or as
`Docx.set_tracking` set it.
