= Read a document's comments

This chapter reads the comments reviewers leave on a document: who wrote
each one and when, what it says, the text it is about, and the thread it
belongs to, and how they are written back. It continues from
<<read-a-documents-text>>, whose blocks and current text it builds on.

== The comments

`Docx.comments` gives a document's comments, read from the part the main
document's comments relationship leads to and joined with the body that
anchors them:

[source,roc]
----
{ document, diagnostics: _ } = Docx.parse(bytes)?
summaries = Docx.comments(document).comments.map(
    |entry|
        match entry.anchor {
            Range(text) => "${entry.comment.author} on: ${text}"
            Point => entry.comment.author
            Unanchored => entry.comment.author
        },
)
----

Each entry is a `Comments.Anchored` record with three fields:

* `comment`, a `Document.Comment`;
* `anchor`, where the comment applies in the body;
* `id`, the comment's place among the comments of the comments part, which
  is how the body names it.

Comments come in the order their anchors begin in the body, as a reader
meets them; comments that start at the same place, such as a comment and its
replies, keep the order the document gives them. Comments the body does not
anchor come last, in the order of their part. A document without a comments
part has no comments.

== What a comment holds

A `Document.Comment` has:

* `author`, the name of the person who wrote it;
* `initials`, `Initials(text)` as the document gives them, or `Unset`;
* `date`, `Date(timestamp)` or `Unset`;
* `content`, its paragraphs and tables as a list of blocks, read the way
  the body's blocks are, so `Document.paragraph_text` gives a paragraph's
  text;
* `preserved`, the comment's other attributes, kept as they were read.

A date is a `Document.Timestamp`: `written`, the date and time as the
document writes it, such as `2026-05-04T09:15:00Z`, and `utc_seconds`, the
instant it names in seconds since 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z. A date with an
offset from UTC is converted; a date without a zone is taken as UTC, as
LibreOffice and pandoc take it. Fractions of a second are left out of the
instant and kept in what was written. A date that is not a date and time
is `Unset`, and the attribute that held it is kept in `preserved`.

The schema requires an author. A comment without one has the empty author,
and a `Defaulted({ element: "comment", attribute: "author" })` diagnostic
comes beside the comments.

Word writes a comment's content beginning with a mark that refers back to
the comment; it is kept as `Preserved` run content and contributes no text.

== Anchors

A comment's `anchor` is one of:

* `Range(text)`, the current text between the start and the end of the
  comment's range, as `Docx.text` reads it: inserted text included, deleted
  and moved-away text not, a line feed for every paragraph that ends inside
  the range. A range that starts and ends at one place, as Word writes a
  comment on no text, covers the empty text. A range that never ends runs to
  the end of the body.
* `Point`, where the body refers to the comment without a range around the
  reference, as Word allows.
* `Unanchored`, when the body neither marks a range for the comment nor
  refers to it.

In the body, a range's boundaries are `CommentStart(id)` and
`CommentEnd(id)`. They are inlines inside a paragraph, and blocks, table
content or row content when a range starts or ends between paragraphs,
rows or cells. The reference that shows where a comment is, usually just
after its range, is the run content `CommentReference(id)`. Each names the
comment by its `id`; the document's own numbers for its comments are not
part of the model.

Neither the boundaries nor the references contribute text. A boundary or
reference that names a comment the comments part does not have, or that
another construct has displaced, is kept as `Preserved`.

== Threads

A comment is a node of a thread. Besides what it says, a
`Document.Comment` has:

* `parent`, `Parent(id)` when the comment is a reply, naming the comment it
  replies to by its place among the comments, or `Unset`;
* `resolved`, whether the comment is marked done;
* `durable_id`, `DurableId(number)`, the identifier that stays with the
  comment across editing sessions, or `Unset`;
* `utc_date`, `Date(timestamp)`, when the comment was written as Word
  records it in UTC, or `Unset`.

A thread starts at a comment without a parent, and a reply's replies follow
their parents up to it. `resolved` is each comment's own mark, as the
document gives it:

[source,roc]
----
entries = Docx.comments(document).comments
open_threads = entries.keep_if(
    |entry| entry.comment.parent == Unset and !entry.comment.resolved,
)
replies_to = |id| entries.keep_if(|entry| entry.comment.parent == Parent(id))
----

The comments part does not say any of this. Word keeps it in four parts
beside it, which the reader joins with the comments: the extended comments
part gives each comment's parent and whether it is done, the comment
identifiers part its durable identifier, and the extensible comments part
the UTC date for a durable identifier. Each knows a comment by the
identifier Word gives the last paragraph of its content, which is not part
of the model. An entry that names no comment, a parent that is not another
comment, and a durable identifier or date that is not one are not applied.
Each entry the model does not hold exactly as it was written, those and an
entry an identifier's later entry replaces or one with anything else in it,
comes back as a `NotApplied({ element, key })` diagnostic beside the
comments, naming the entry (`commentEx`, `commentId` or
`commentExtensible`) and the paragraph or durable identifier it names, and
is written back as it was read.

A document without these parts, as LibreOffice, Google Docs and pandoc
write one, has the same comments with every `parent` and `durable_id` and
`utc_date` `Unset` and none resolved.

== People

`Docx.people` gives the people part of a document as `people`: the
authors of its comments and tracked changes as Word records them, each a
`Document.Person` with:

* `author`, the name the comments and changes give as their author;
* `presence`, `Presence({ provider, user })`, the identity provider that
  knows the author and their identifier there, or `Unset`.

A presence without both a provider and an identifier is `Unset`, with a
`NotApplied({ element: "presenceInfo", key: author })` diagnostic in
`diagnostics`, and is written back as it was read. A document without a
people part has no people.

== Write comments

`Docx.to_bytes` writes a document's comments from the model, as it writes
its body: the comments part from the comments `Docx.comments` reads, and
each of the extended comments, comment identifiers, extensible comments and
people parts the document has from the threads and people. So what a
comment says, who wrote it, when, what it replies to and whether it is
resolved is written as the model holds it, and Word and LibreOffice show
replies as replies and resolved comments as resolved.

* Each comment is written with its author, date, initials and the other
  attributes it kept, and its content is written as the body's blocks are,
  in the same canonical form.
* Comments take new identifiers, as <<write-a-document>> says, and every
  range and reference to a comment takes its comment's.
* The last paragraph of each comment's content is given a paragraph
  identifier, `w14:paraId`, the lowest from `00000001` up that no other
  part and no paragraph kept in a comment uses. The other parts name the
  comment by it.
* The extended comments part has an entry for each comment with a
  paragraph: the paragraph of the comment it replies to, and whether it is
  done. The comment identifiers part has an entry for each such comment
  with a durable identifier, and the extensible comments part one for that
  durable identifier with the comment's UTC date where it has one, keeping
  whatever else the document's entry for that durable identifier held,
  such as reactions.
* The people part has each person with their presence.

Each part keeps its root's declarations and attributes and anything it
holds that is not one of its entries. The entries reading did not apply,
each a `NotApplied` diagnostic, are written as they were read, before the
written entries so that they never stand in for them, and the paragraph
identifiers they name are not given to any comment, so they name no
comment when the document is read again. A person keeps what else the
document wrote for them, and the presence reading did not apply. A comment
whose content has no paragraph cannot be named by the other parts: one
with a thread to write, a parent, replies, the resolved mark, a durable
identifier or a UTC date, cannot be written, and `Docx.to_bytes` fails
with `CommentWithoutParagraph(id)`; one without is written as it is. A
UTC date is kept by its durable identifier, so a comment without one is
written without its UTC date.
