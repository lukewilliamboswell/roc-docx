= Generate a document

This chapter starts a new document instead of reading one: what a new
document holds, how its content is built, which styles and lists it has,
and how its properties are given. It continues from <<write-a-document>>,
which says how a document is written.

== Start a new document

`Docx.new_document` builds a document from the default document kit:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=new-document,indent=0]
----

Its argument is the `Docx.Properties` record that `Docx.set_properties`
takes (<<set-what-a-document-says-about-itself>>): it names the document's
title, its author (`creator`), when it was created and last modified
(`modified`) and any other core property, and each can be left out:
`Docx.new_document({})` is a document without properties. The package reads
no clock, so a document has only the dates its caller gives it, and the same
properties always give the same bytes. A date is in the W3C date and time
format: a year, a month or a day, or a day and a time with its time zone,
such as `2026-11-10T09:00:00Z` or `2026-11-10T19:00:00+10:00`; any other is
refused with `NotADate` and the date as given.

The new document is an empty page: one empty paragraph on A4 paper, portrait,
with one-inch margins and the header and footer half an inch from the edge
of the page. Word and LibreOffice open it as a blank document, and it reads
back like any other: `Docx.text` gives one empty line, `Docx.styles` the
styles below, `Docx.numbering` the lists, and `Docx.core_properties` the
properties it was given.

== Build the content

The `Build` module makes the content: paragraphs, text, list items, tables
and links are values of the document model, and the rest, the notes,
headers and footers, pages and properties, are added to the document. Each
takes its content and a record of options, every option with a default, so
`{}` asks for none:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=build,indent=0]
----

`Build.append` adds blocks at the end of the body, before the end of its
final section; the one empty paragraph of a new document gives way to the
first blocks appended.

`Build.paragraph` sets a paragraph's `style`, the normal style by default, a
heading's with `Heading(1)` to `Heading(6)` or any style by its identifier
with `Style("ListParagraph")`; its `alignment`, `Left`, `Center`, `Right` or
`Justified`; and whether it starts on a new page, `page_break_before: On`.

`Build.text` makes a run of text. A tab in the text is a tab and a line feed
a line break. Its options set `bold`, `italic`, `underline` and `strike`,
each `On`, or `Off` against what a style sets; `vertical_align`,
`Superscript` or `Subscript`; `size` in half-points, `HalfPoints(24)` being 12
points; `color`, `Rgb({ red: 192, green: 0, blue: 0 })`; `font`,
`Named("Cambria")`; and a character `style`. What a run leaves `Unset`
comes from the paragraph's style and the document's defaults.

`Build.list_item` makes an item of the bulleted list, or with
`list: Numbered` of the numbered one, at its `level` from 0, in the List
Paragraph style; the lists are described below.

`Build.link` makes a link to an address around its content; runs without a
character style of their own take the Hyperlink style. The relationship
that holds the address is added when the document is written.

`Build.table` makes a table in the Table Grid style from rows of cells made
by `Build.cell`. Its `widths` are its columns' in twips (1,440 to the inch),
and without them the columns share the width of an A4 page between one-inch
margins; its first `header_rows` rows repeat at the top of each page it runs
onto. A cell `span`s grid columns, and is as wide as the columns it spans; a
cell with `merge: Restart` starts a vertical merge that the cells below it
with `merge: Continue` join. A cell ends with a paragraph, so an empty one is
given one.

== Notes, headers and footers

`Build.footnote` adds a footnote and gives back the document with it and the
reference to place in a paragraph, which shows the note's number. The note
is a paragraph in the footnote text style starting with its number. A
document without footnotes is given a footnotes part with the separator
lines drawn above the notes.

`Build.header` and `Build.footer` give the final section a header or
footer, from blocks as the body's are, in a part of their own that is added
with its relationship; giving one again replaces it. With `{}` it is for
every page. A header or footer ends with a paragraph, so an empty one is
given an empty paragraph.
`Build.page_number` and `Build.page_count` are fields for the page's number
and the number of pages, which Word and LibreOffice calculate as they lay
the document out:

=== Odd, even and first pages

A book-like document often puts a different header on left and right
pages, and leaves the first page of a section blank or gives it a title of
its own. The options' `kind` says which pages a header or footer is for:
`Default`, the default, `Even` for even pages, or `First` for the section's
first page:

[source,roc]
----
include::../tests/manual.roc[tag=odd-even-first,indent=0]
----

An `Even` header or footer turns on different odd and even pages, which is
a setting of the whole document, as `Docx.set_different_odd_and_even`
turns it on; the `Default` ones are then for odd pages, and a section with
no `Even` one of its own shows the previous section's on its even pages. A
`First` one turns on the section's title page, so its first page shows it
instead of the `Default` one. A new section starts without a title page and
without headers and footers of its own, showing the previous section's
until it is given its own, of any kind.

== Pages and sections

`Build.page_setup` sets the final section's page: its `size`, `A4`,
`Letter`, `Legal` or `Size({ width, height })` in twips; its `orientation`,
`Portrait` or `Landscape`; its `top`, `right`, `bottom` and `left` margins
and its `header` and `footer` distances from the edge of the page, in
twips; and where it `start`s, `NextPage`, `Continuous`, `EvenPage` or
`OddPage`. The defaults are the new document's page.

`Build.new_section` ends the final section where the body ends, keeping its
page, header and footer, and starts a new one with the page its setup gives.
The new section shows the header and footer of the section before it until
it is given its own:

== Properties

`Build.properties` sets the document's title, subject, author (`creator`),
keywords, description, who last modified it, its revision number and when it
was created and last modified, as `Docx.set_properties` sets them on any
document (<<set-what-a-document-says-about-itself>>). A property given
replaces the document's, and one left out keeps it; a document without core
properties is given them. A date that is not in the W3C date and time
format is refused with `NotADate`, as `Docx.new_document` refuses it.

== The styles

The document's text is Calibri at 11 points, with 8 points after each
paragraph and lines at 1.08 times their height, as Word sets its own
defaults. Each style has the identifier and the name Word gives its
built-in style, which is how Word and LibreOffice recognise it as their
own: they show it under its name in their own language, and LibreOffice
maps it onto its corresponding style, such as _Heading 1_ or
_Internet link_.

[cols="2,2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Identifier |Name |For
|`Normal` |Normal |Every paragraph without a style of its own; the default
|`Heading1` to `Heading6` |heading 1 to heading 6 |Headings at outline levels 1 to 6, kept with the next paragraph, followed by a normal paragraph
|`ListParagraph` |List Paragraph |A list item, indented half an inch
|`DefaultParagraphFont` |Default Paragraph Font |Text without a character style; the default
|`Hyperlink` |Hyperlink |A link's text, blue and underlined
|`TableNormal` |Normal Table |Every table without a style of its own; the default
|`TableGrid` |Table Grid |A table with a thin line around every cell
|`NoList` |No List |The default numbering style
|`FootnoteText`, `EndnoteText` |footnote text, endnote text |A note's text, single-spaced at 10 points
|`FootnoteReference`, `EndnoteReference` |footnote reference, endnote reference |A note's number, superscript
|`CommentText` |annotation text |A comment's text, single-spaced at 10 points
|`CommentReference` |annotation reference |Where a comment is marked in the text
|===

A paragraph or run names a style by its identifier, as
`Document.ParagraphProperties` and `Document.RunProperties` hold it.

== The lists

The document has two lists, each of nine levels, the levels indented half an
inch apiece with the bullet or number hanging a quarter inch before the
text:

* numbering instance 1 is bulleted, with a bullet, a circle and a square in turn from the
  first level down;
* numbering instance 2 is numbered `1.`, `a.`, `i.` in turn from the first
  level down, each level counting from 1.

A paragraph is an item of a list when its properties name the instance and
the level, from 0 for the first, as `Document.Numbering` holds them.

== The parts of a new document

A new document's package has the parts a WordprocessingML package needs and
no others: the main document, the styles, the numbering definitions, the
settings (tab stops every half inch, and Word's current layout rather than
an earlier version's compatibility mode), the font table, and the core
properties, each with its relationship and content type.

The styles, the list definitions and the core properties are written from
the model by `WmlWrite.styles`, `WmlWrite.numbering` and
`WmlWrite.core_properties`, in the canonical form <<write-a-document>>
describes, so what they hold is what `Styles.read`, `Numbering.read` and
`WmlRead.core_properties` read back. Each writer takes model values on its
own and gives the part's tree:

[source,roc]
----

A style keeps the elements the model does not hold, such as its place in
the style gallery or a table style's table properties, and a level and a
numbering definition theirs, each written in the place the schema gives it.
The settings and the font table have no model, and are built as trees. Once
the document is built, every part but the main document is written from its
tree, as in any document that was read.
